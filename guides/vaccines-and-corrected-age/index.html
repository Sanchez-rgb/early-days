Vaccines and corrected age

Updated 23 November 2026

Vaccines are one of the main exceptions to the corrected-age rule. The schedule generally follows chronological age, not the due date.

The short answer

Vaccines generally follow chronological age. Corrected age is used for growth and developmental milestones, not for the standard vaccination schedule.

Your pediatrician may adjust timing for a specific vaccine or recommend extra protection for a premature baby. That is a clinical decision, not something to calculate from the due date.

Why chronological age

Vaccine schedules are built around the immune system's response at a given time since birth, and around the risk of exposure. Premature babies are often vaccinated on the same schedule as full-term babies, because they are at least as vulnerable to infections.

What to bring to the visit

  • Birth date and due date.
  • Gestational age at birth and any NICU course details.
  • The vaccination record from the hospital or NICU.
  • Any reaction your baby had to a previous vaccine.

Questions worth asking

  • Is my baby on the standard schedule, or does their history change anything?
  • Are there extra protections recommended for premature babies?
  • What should we watch for after this vaccine?

Questions

Do vaccines use corrected age?

Generally no. The standard schedule uses chronological age. Ask your pediatrician about your baby's specific plan.

Are preemies vaccinated later?

Many are vaccinated on the same schedule. Some clinicians adjust for specific circumstances; follow their advice.

What if my baby is still in the NICU?

The NICU team will advise on timing. Bring the question to them rather than deciding from a general guide.
