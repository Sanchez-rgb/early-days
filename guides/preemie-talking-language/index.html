When do preemies talk?

Updated 23 November 2026

Language develops on a wide range. Corrected age is the fair clock for a premature baby, especially in the first two years.

A rough timeline

StageCommon age (corrected)What it looks like
Cooing2–4 monthsOpen vowel sounds, back-and-forth with you.
Babbling6–9 months“mamamama,” “babababa,” raspberries.
First wordsAround 12 months“mama,” “dada,” a consistent label.
Two-word phrasesAround 24 months“more milk,” “daddy gone.”

These are rough ranges, not deadlines. Understanding usually comes before speaking.

Bilingual homes

If you speak more than one language at home, count words in both languages. Mixing languages in one sentence is normal for bilingual children and is not a sign of confusion.

When to ask

  • No babbling by about 9 months corrected.
  • No words by about 16–18 months corrected.
  • No two-word phrases by about 24 months corrected.
  • Loss of words or social sounds they previously used.

These are general prompts, not diagnostic criteria. If you are worried, ask your pediatrician about a speech-language evaluation.

Questions

Does corrected age apply to speech?

In the first two years, yes. Many clinicians assess language at corrected age for premature babies.

Is it normal for a preemie to talk late?

Some preemies talk later and catch up. A pattern of delay or a loss of skills is worth discussing.

Does screen time affect talking?

Language grows through back-and-forth interaction. Ask your pediatrician for guidance on screen time at your baby's age.
