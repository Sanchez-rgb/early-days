Corrected age at 1 year and 2 years

Updated 23 November 2026

At 12 months corrected, your baby's actual age is 12 months plus the weeks they were born early. At 24 months, many pediatricians stop correcting.

At 12 months corrected

At 12 months corrected, actual age is roughly 12 months plus the weeks early. A baby born 8 weeks early is about 13.8 months actual at 12 months corrected.

Weeks earlyActual age at 12 months corrected
4 weeks≈ 12.9 months
8 weeks≈ 13.8 months
12 weeks≈ 14.8 months

At 24 months corrected

At 24 months corrected, a baby born 12 weeks early is about 26.8 months actual. This is the age when many pediatricians switch to chronological age for growth and milestones, though some continue adjusted-age screening to 30 months.

After the cutoff

School enrollment, legal ages, and most paperwork use chronological age. Prematurity history may still matter in developmental evaluations, but that is a separate question from which age to write on a milestone checklist.

Ask your pediatrician when to stop correcting. The right cutoff depends on how early your baby was born and what they are tracking.

Questions

Do I correct at 1 year?

Many pediatricians still use corrected age at 12 months, especially for babies born very early. Ask yours.

Do I correct at 2 years?

Many stop around 24 months; some continue to 30 months for screening. Follow your pediatrician.

Does school use corrected age?

No. School enrollment and most legal ages use chronological age.
