Corrected age chart (0–24 months)

Updated 23 November 2026

A quick reference for the question every preemie parent asks: if my baby is 6 months corrected, how old are they actually? The answer depends on how many weeks early they were born.

How to read the chart

Corrected age is measured from the due date. Actual age is measured from the birth date. The difference is how many weeks early your baby was born.

Actual age = corrected age + weeks born early

Months are approximate because a month is not a fixed number of days. Use the calculator for an exact answer in weeks and days.

Two worked examples

Born at 28 weeks (12 weeks early). At 6 months corrected, actual age is about 6 + 2.8 = 8.8 months. The baby is not “6 months behind”; they are on a different calendar.

Born at 34 weeks (6 weeks early). At 9 months corrected, actual age is about 9 + 1.4 = 10.4 months.

Using the chart with your pediatrician

Bring both ages to checkups: “14 months actual, 11 months corrected.” That one sentence saves the clinician from recalculating and makes the milestone conversation clearer.

This chart is general information. Growth and development are clinical judgements; ask your pediatrician which age they want you to use.

Questions

Is the chart exact?

No. Months are approximate because months have different lengths. The calculator gives an exact corrected age in weeks and days.

Do I use corrected age for growth charts?

In the first two years, growth is commonly plotted at corrected age, using Fenton or WHO charts depending on the baby's age and your clinician's approach.

When do I stop using corrected age?

Many pediatricians stop around 24 months; some continue adjusted-age screening to 30 months, and very preterm babies may be corrected to 36 months.
