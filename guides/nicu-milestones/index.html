NICU milestones

Updated 22 November 2026

NICU progress is not a straight line. Babies move forward, pause, and sometimes take a step back. The milestones below are skills the team is watching for — not a schedule you can hurry.

Progress is not linear

A baby may come off CPAP, go back on it for a few days, and then come off again. That is common and does not mean the first attempt failed. The NICU team is looking at trends, not a single day. Ask “what are we working toward this week?” rather than “when will she be off?” — the first question gets you a plan, the second gets you a guess.

Breathing support

Breathing support often moves in steps: a ventilator, then CPAP, then a lower-flow cannula, then room air. Not every baby follows every step, and some skip or repeat stages. The milestone is not a specific device; it is stable breathing with less support over time.

Ask the team which support your baby is on, what the next step would look like, and what they need to see before trying it.

Feeding

Early on, nutrition may come through an IV (TPN) and then through a feeding tube. As the gut matures, feeds advance. The big feeding milestones are:

  • First oral feed. A first attempt at breast or bottle, often small and messy.
  • Full oral feeds. All feeds by mouth, with the feeding tube out.

Oral feeding is hard work. A baby has to coordinate sucking, swallowing, and breathing, and stay awake long enough to finish. That coordination typically develops around 32–34 weeks, but practice is what builds stamina. “Full oral feeds” is often one of the last milestones before discharge, and some babies go home with a partial tube-feeding plan.

Do not compare your baby's feeding timeline to another baby in the room. Gestational age, breathing history, and stamina all change the pace.

Open crib and temperature

Moving from an incubator or warmer to an open crib is a temperature-control milestone. The baby needs to hold their own temperature in a normal room. It often happens after breathing and feeding are stable, but the order can vary. Ask what your baby needs to show before the move.

Discharge

Discharge criteria vary by hospital and by baby. Common pieces include:

  • Stable breathing without major support
  • Reliable feeding and weight gain
  • Stable temperature in an open crib
  • Newborn screening, hearing screen, and any required tests completed
  • A car seat test for babies who need it
  • Parents trained on feeding, safe sleep, and what to watch for
  • Follow-up appointments arranged

Some babies go home on oxygen, a monitor, or a feeding plan. “Coming home” does not always mean “no equipment.” Ask the team to write down the discharge plan and who to call with questions.

How to track without pressure

  • Write down the milestone when it happens, not the date you expected it.
  • Keep a short list of questions for rounds instead of trying to remember everything.
  • Record the small wins — first kangaroo care, first oral feed, first day in an open crib. They are real progress.
  • Use one place for the milestones so you are not reconstructing the timeline from memory later.

Early Days includes a NICU milestone list — ventilator off, full oral feeds, first kangaroo care, graduated from the NICU — alongside the usual home milestones. It records what happened; it does not score your baby.

Questions

What are the main NICU milestones?

Common milestones include coming off breathing support, coming off IV nutrition, advancing feeds, taking full oral feeds, moving from an incubator to an open crib, and passing discharge checks. The order and timing vary by baby.

When can a premature baby come home?

Discharge criteria vary by hospital and baby. They usually include stable breathing, reliable feeding and weight gain, stable temperature in an open crib, and completion of screening and parent education. Ask your baby's team what the current goals are.

Is full oral feeding the last milestone?

Often it is one of the last, because feeding is complex and babies need to coordinate sucking, swallowing, and breathing. Some babies go home with a feeding tube or a plan for partial tube feeds. Follow your team's plan.

Can I do kangaroo care in the NICU?

Many NICUs encourage skin-to-skin care when the baby is stable enough. Ask your baby's nurse about timing, positioning, and any precautions for your baby.
