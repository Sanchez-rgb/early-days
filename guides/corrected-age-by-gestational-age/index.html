Corrected age by gestational age

Updated 23 November 2026

Find your baby's gestational age at birth, and you can see how many weeks early they were and what that means for corrected age.

Find the gestational age

Gestational age at birth is the number of weeks pregnant you were when your baby was born. A full-term pregnancy is about 40 weeks. The difference is how early your baby arrived.

Weeks early = 40 − gestational age at birth

“3 months actual” is about 91 days. If the corrected age is negative, your baby is still before the due date and corrected age is a countdown, not a negative age.

What this means day to day

Use corrected age for growth and milestones in the first two years. Use chronological age for vaccines and paperwork.

A 32-weeker is 8 weeks early. At 6 months corrected, they are about 7.8 months actual. That gap matters when someone hands you a full-term milestone checklist.

Questions

Does this apply to late preterm babies?

Yes. Late preterm babies (34–36 weeks) are still premature, though the offset is smaller. Ask your pediatrician whether they want you to correct.

What if my baby was born at 28 weeks?

That is 12 weeks early. At 3 months actual, corrected age is about 1 week.

What if my baby was born at 36 weeks?

That is 4 weeks early. At 3 months actual, corrected age is about 9 weeks.
