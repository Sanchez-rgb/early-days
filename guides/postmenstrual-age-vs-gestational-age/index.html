Postmenstrual age vs gestational age

Updated 23 November 2026

The NICU uses several ages at once. Here is what each one means, and how they relate to corrected age.

The four ages

TermWhat it meansExample
Gestational age (GA)Weeks pregnant at birthBorn at 28 weeks
Chronological ageTime since birth3 months old
Postmenstrual age (PMA)GA at birth + weeks since birth28 + 12 = 40 weeks PMA
Corrected ageAge from the due dateAbout 1 week corrected

Postmenstrual age

Postmenstrual age is the age measured from the first day of the last menstrual period, as if the baby were still in the womb. It is gestational age at birth plus the weeks since birth.

PMA = gestational age at birth + weeks since birth

At 40 weeks PMA, a baby is at term-equivalent age and corrected age is zero. The Fenton preterm growth charts cover about 22½ to 50 weeks PMA.

Why the NICU uses PMA

PMA gives the care team one clock that works before and after birth. It is used for growth charts, feeding readiness, and discharge planning. Parents usually hear corrected age after discharge, but it is the same idea expressed in weeks.

Questions

Is postmenstrual age the same as corrected age?

They are related but not identical. At 40 weeks PMA, corrected age is zero. After that, corrected age and postmenstrual age move together, but PMA is usually expressed in weeks.

What is term-equivalent age?

About 40 weeks PMA. It is the point a baby would have reached the due date.

Why do the Fenton charts stop at 50 weeks?

The charts are designed for preterm infants through early infancy. After that, clinicians usually switch to WHO growth standards plotted at corrected age.
