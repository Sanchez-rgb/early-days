When do preemies crawl and walk?

Updated 23 November 2026

Crawling and walking have wide ranges. For a premature baby, corrected age is the fair comparison — and some babies skip crawling entirely.

On this page

Common ranges

SkillCommon range (corrected age)Notes
Rolling4–6 monthsOne direction first is common.
Sitting without support6–9 monthsSome preemies sit later.
Crawling6–10 monthsSome babies skip it or bottom-shuffle.
Pulling to stand9–12 monthsCruising often follows.
Walking9–18 monthsA wide range is normal.

Why corrected age matters

A baby born 10 weeks early who walks at 14 months actual is walking at about 11.7 months corrected. That is well within the usual range. Comparing to the birthday makes a preemie look late when they are not.

When to ask

  • Strong one-sided preference, stiffness, or floppiness.
  • Not bearing weight on legs by about 9 months corrected.
  • Not walking by about 18 months corrected.
  • Loss of a movement skill they previously had.

Questions

Do all babies crawl?

No. Some bottom-shuffle, commando-crawl, or skip crawling and go straight to pulling up.

Is walking at 18 months too late?

Many preemies walk later than full-term babies. Ask your pediatrician if you are worried, especially if other movement skills are also delayed.

Should I use a walker?

Ask your pediatrician. Some clinicians discourage walkers; supervised floor play and cruising practice are often preferred.
