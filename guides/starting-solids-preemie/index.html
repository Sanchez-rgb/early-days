Starting solids for a preemie

Updated 23 November 2026

Starting solids is about readiness signs, not a birthday. For premature babies, many pediatricians use corrected age when discussing timing.

The timing question

Many pediatricians discuss solid foods around 4–6 months corrected, once a baby shows readiness signs. Some preemies, especially those with feeding difficulties, need a different plan.

Do not start solids based on a chart alone. Ask your pediatrician, and involve a feeding therapist if your baby has a history of feeding problems.

Readiness signs

  • Good head and neck control.
  • Can sit with support and hold their head up.
  • Tongue-thrust reflex is fading (food stays in, not pushed straight out).
  • Shows interest in food and opens their mouth for a spoon.
  • Can turn their head away to show they are done.

What to expect

Early meals are practice, not nutrition. Milk remains the main source of calories for months. Expect mess, small amounts, and days when your baby is not interested.

If your baby was born very early or had feeding therapy, ask whether the plan should be adjusted for their corrected age and their individual feeding history.

Questions

Do I use corrected age for solids?

Many pediatricians do, but readiness signs matter more than the number. Ask your pediatrician.

Should I start with cereal?

There are several acceptable first foods. The best choice depends on your baby's needs and your family's diet; ask your pediatrician.

What if my baby gags?

Gagging is not the same as choking, but feeding concerns deserve a professional look. Ask your pediatrician or a feeding therapist.
