Corrected age for twins and multiples

Updated 23 November 2026

Twins and multiples usually share a due date, but each baby has their own NICU course, growth curve, and developmental timeline.

One due date, two babies

If your twins share a due date, they share the same corrected-age starting line. But they may have been born at different weights, had different breathing support, or spent different time in the NICU.

Track each baby individually

  • Use each baby's own growth curve, not a shared chart.
  • Compare each baby to their own corrected age, not to their twin.
  • Bring separate milestone notes to checkups.
  • Expect different timelines; that is normal, not a competition.

When to ask

If one twin is consistently behind the other across several areas, or loses a skill, raise it with the pediatrician. The comparison to watch is each baby's own trajectory, not the gap between them.

Twins are not a controlled experiment. Birth order, birth weight, and NICU course all differ. Use corrected age as the common clock, not as a scoreboard.

Questions

Do twins have the same corrected age?

If they share a due date, yes. The corrected age is measured from that due date for both.

Should I compare twins?

Compare each baby to their own curve and corrected age, not to each other.

What if one twin was born earlier?

Use each baby's own birth date and the shared due date. Ask your pediatrician if the situation is unusual.
