What corrected age means

A baby born early has two ages. Chronological age counts from birth. Corrected age counts from the due date. For growth, feeding, and developmental milestones in the first two years, pediatricians usually use corrected age — because that is the timeline your baby's body was actually developing on.

Counting from birth makes a preemie look behind. Counting from the due date shows what is actually happening. Nothing about your baby changes; only the starting line does.

Example

A baby born at 28 weeks — 12 weeks before the due date — is now 4 months old (about 122 days). Corrected age = 122 − 84 = 38 days, or about 5 weeks 3 days. The baby is not “4 months behind”; they are a 5-week-old in a 4-month-old's calendar slot.

How long do you use corrected age?

Many pediatricians use corrected age until about 24 months, and some extend it to 36 months for very premature babies. Ask your own pediatrician — the cutoff varies with how early your baby was born.

This calculator only does arithmetic. It does not assess growth or development. If you are worried about your baby, contact your pediatrician.

Questions

Is corrected age the same as adjusted age?

Yes. Corrected age, adjusted age, and corrected gestational age all describe the same idea: age measured from the original due date instead of the birth date.

Do vaccines use corrected age?

No. Vaccination schedules generally use chronological age. Ask your pediatrician if you are unsure.

My baby was born after the due date. What then?

Then the baby was not premature, and corrected age equals actual age. The calculator will show that directly.

Why does the result say “before the due date”?

If your baby is already born but the due date has not arrived yet, corrected age is negative. Early Days shows a countdown to the due date instead of a negative age.
